Analysis

The national count is substantial

India recorded 29,670 rape cases in the NCRB's 2023 table. That is a count of cases registered with authorities, so it should not be treated as a complete measure of the actual prevalence of sexual violence.

The burden is geographically concentrated

Rajasthan had the highest absolute count at 5,078 cases. The four highest-count States — Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra — together represented approximately 48.9% of the national total.

A relatively small group accounts for most cases

The ten highest-count States and UTs recorded 22,066 cases, equal to approximately 74.4% of India's registered cases in this table. This is a concentration of recorded cases, not a statement about the underlying risk of rape in those jurisdictions.

Circumstances should be read carefully

The NCRB table contains legal and circumstance-specific categories, including repeated rape, rape involving persons in positions of trust, rape of women below specified ages and gang rape. These categories should not automatically be added together to produce a new national total because the categories may describe overlapping characteristics of cases.

How to read this dashboard

Important context before drawing conclusions from the numbers.

  • Registered cases ≠ total prevalence. These numbers represent cases recorded by authorities.
  • National figures use the NCRB's "Total All India" row. The dataset also contains "Total State (S)" and "Total UT (S)" aggregate rows, which are excluded from state rankings.
  • State ranking uses individual jurisdictions only. Aggregate rows such as Total State, Total UT and Total All India are never treated as states.
  • Categories are not a second total. The circumstance/legal-section columns provide additional context and should not be summed blindly.
  • Absolute counts are not rates. A state with more registered cases does not necessarily have a higher per-capita incidence. Population, reporting, access to police, registration practices and other factors matter.
